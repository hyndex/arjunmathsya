HOW HARDWARE INTEGRATION WORKS
## Integration is more
 than a shared enclosure.
 01
### Control commonization
Review shared compute, mode coordination, diagnostic services and reusable embedded software. Keep real-time and supervisory responsibilities defined.
 02
### Power & interfaces
Retain, adapt or develop OBC, DC/DC and distribution functions against the approved reference architecture and available supplier interfaces.
 03
### Physical realization
Evaluate housing, cooling, isolation, connectors and mechanical integration. A shared housing does not prove a lower cost or simpler qualification.
 04
### System evidence
Verify modes, timing, fault response, thermal behavior, EMC and the complete prototype, integration and qualification cost.
 Common control and AI supervision have separate proof obligations.
Hardware commonization must earn its cost case. The model must separately demonstrate useful insight within measured compute and latency limits. Validated protection and fault containment remain part of the architecture.
{id=benefits}
THE HARDWARE VALUE CASE
## Fewer unnecessary boundaries.
 More reusable evidence.
 CUSTOMER / VEHICLE
### Coordinated operating behavior
Evaluate charging readiness, power/thermal explanations and useful health guidance through the approved vehicle interfaces.
 Measure readiness, energy use in a defined test, false recommendations and service outcomes.
 OEM / PRODUCT ENGINEERING
### A platform that can be reused
Explore shared control and software across defined power functions, with consistent diagnostics and linked operating evidence.
 Measure controller/interface count, BOM, NRE, validation effort, software reuse and variant cost.
 SERVICE / QUALITY
### Context for a power-system event
Link the module observation to operating mode, cooling context and hardware/software version. Compare behavior after an approved correction.
 Measure triage time, evidence completeness, no-fault-found outcomes and recurrence.
 BUSINESS CASE
### The complete cost of integration
Compare the integrated concept with the reference design, including redesign, tooling, supplier access, qualification, repairability and lifecycle support.
 Measured net value, rather than controller count alone, decides the next generation.

A SEPARATE HARDWARE DEVELOPMENT PATH
## From architecture.
 To a program-ready platform.
 01
### Reference
Vehicle scope, power ratings, interfaces, retained functions and responsibilities.
 02
### Bench
Controlled modes, interface timing, fault response and AI runtime feasibility.
 03
### Prototype
Power-stage realization, cooling, packaging, EMC and a complete cost model.
 04
### Qualify
Partner-defined vehicle integration, qualification and lifecycle readiness.
Hardware generations have no committed release dates. The 12–16 week plan elsewhere on the site is a scoped PMG software PoC, not a production-hardware delivery promise.
